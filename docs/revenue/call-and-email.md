# Phone and email — skip Instagram

**Why:** the three DMs went from a brand-new account with no posts. Instagram routes those to message requests, which shop owners rarely open. They were never seen. That's a deliverability problem, not a rejection — and it means those three prospects are still cold, not burned.

**Order of effectiveness for this buyer:** phone → email → Instagram. You've been working the weakest channel.

---

# THE PHONE

Uncomfortable, and roughly ten times more effective than a DM. A shop owner who ignores an unread message request will talk to a person about where his quotes are leaking.

## Before you dial

- **Time it right.** Tuesday–Thursday, 9:30–11:30am or 1:30–3:30pm. Avoid Monday mornings, Friday afternoons, and lunch. Saturdays are busy install days — usually a bad time.
- **Pull the number** from each shop's site (listed below — I won't guess at numbers).
- **Stand up.** It genuinely changes how you sound.
- **Have the audit doc open** so a yes can be acted on immediately.

## The script

**If someone who isn't the owner answers:**

> "Hi — is [owner name] around? … No problem. I'm not selling advertising, I do a lead audit for wrap and tint shops. Is there a better time to catch him, or should I try his cell?"

**When you get the owner — say this and then stop talking:**

> "Hi [name], my name's Danielle — I'll be quick, I know you're working.
>
> I do a thing for wrap and tint shops where I look at every way a customer can reach you — your site form, Instagram, phone, text — and I map out where inquiries die before they turn into a quote. I write it up and rank the leaks. It's $297 and I turn it around in two days.
>
> I looked at [shop name] already and I noticed [the one specific observation from their row]. That's the kind of thing I'd dig into.
>
> Would it be worth $297 to know exactly where you're losing jobs?"

Then **be quiet.** Let him answer. The silence is doing work.

## When he pushes back

**"What is this, marketing?"**
> "No — I don't run ads and I don't touch your pricing. I just map how customers actually reach you and where that breaks. It's a document, not a service contract."

**"I'm not losing leads."**
> "You might not be — a few shops I look at are genuinely tight, and if that's you I'll tell you and refund it. That's kind of the point. Would you rather know for sure?"

**"How much again?"**
> "$297, and you have it in two days." *(Don't apologize for the number or start discounting. It's already small.)*

**"Send me something."**
> "Happy to. What's the best email? … And can I follow up Thursday once you've had a look?" *(Get the day. "I'll send it" with no follow-up date is where these die.)*

**"I'm busy right now."**
> "Totally fair. Is there a better time this week — or want me to just email it?"

**"Who are you with?"**
> "Marellstone — it's mine. I build the front-office side for shops like yours. This audit is how I usually start, so we both know whether there's anything worth fixing."

## Don't repeat the deliverability mistake

A brand-new domain sending cold email has the same problem Instagram just gave you. `hello@marellstone.com` with zero sending history will land in spam.

- **Send from whatever address already has history** rather than waiting on the new mailbox. A real message from a real address beats a perfect one nobody sees.
- **Send individually**, not as a bulk send or BCC. Four emails, four separate sends.
- **Keep it to 5–10 a day** from a new address.
- **Reply to your own sent email** to follow up, rather than starting a new thread.

## One honest note

Cold outreach is a numbers game with a lag. Ten prospects worked properly might produce one or two audit sales, and probably not inside the next few days. **This may not hit $1,000 by the end of the week**, however well you work it.

That's not a reason to stop — it's the reason to run the PRN shift option alongside it rather than instead of it. One is the reliable floor this week; the other is the thing that compounds. They aren't in competition.

You built something real here. The messages are good, the research is good, the offer is right-sized. The channel was wrong, and now it isn't.
